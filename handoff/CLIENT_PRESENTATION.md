# What was built, and why it is worth having

Plain English. No jargon.

---

## The problem this solves

Right now everything you have lives on Instagram. That works for attention and
badly for everything else.

On Instagram:

- Your five-part first-time-buyer series is scattered across months of posts.
  Nobody watches it in order because nobody can find part two.
- Your best results — the $1,960,000 Brooklyn sale at 98% of list, the Staten
  Island seller two agents had already failed — are screenshots that scroll past
  and disappear.
- Someone deciding whether to call you has to piece your credibility together
  from a grid of thumbnails.
- None of it shows up when someone searches "real estate agent Staten Island".
- You do not own any of it. The platform does.

The website fixes the part Instagram cannot do: **organising attention into a
decision.**

---

## What it does that a feed cannot

### It puts your work in order

The five-part buyer series is now a numbered sequence with a progress marker.
Someone opens part one, and the site remembers where they got to — on their own
device, with no account and no tracking. They finish the series understanding
the process, which means the first conversation you have with them starts three
steps further along.

### It makes your results permanent and checkable

Three transactions are documented as full case studies: the exact property, the
constraint the client was under, what you actually did, the outcome, the
client's own words, and the original review sitting one click away for anyone
who wants to verify it.

That last part matters more than it sounds. Most agent sites show star ratings.
This one shows the receipt.

### It separates buyers from sellers

A buyer and a seller need opposite things from you. The site asks which one they
are within the first screen and sends them somewhere different — buyers to the
budget and the sequence, sellers to pricing, preparation, and the marketing.
Neither has to read the other's page.

### It gets found

Two pages are built specifically for local search — one for Staten Island, one
for Brooklyn — each anchored to transactions that actually closed there. That is
the difference between a page that ranks and a page that gets ignored as
boilerplate.

### It works when someone is standing outside a house

Everything is designed for a phone first, because that is where your audience
already watches you. Call and text are always one tap away at the bottom of the
screen.

## What is specific to you

Take your name off this site and it stops working. That was the test.

- **The structure is your work.** Your output is a numbered series of vertical
  films and a numbered set of closed deals. The site is built as an index — a
  call sheet — because that is the shape your work already has.
- **The proportions are your footage.** Every media frame is 9:16, the shape you
  actually shoot. Nothing is cropped into a landscape box it was never made for.
- **The proof is yours and nobody else's.** 327 Brehaut, 181 Meisner, 2031 60th
  Street. Real addresses, real numbers, real clients.
- **The tone is yours.** Short sentences. The useful answer first. No "your
  trusted partner in real estate."

A template can be resold to the next agent. This cannot — remove your
transactions and the pages stop making sense rather than degrading into
something generic.

## Your films now work harder

Every film you supplied has been:

- converted into a fast web version that plays on any phone,
- given a poster frame so the page looks finished before anything loads,
- **captioned** — so it works with the sound off, which is how most people watch,
- **transcribed** — so the words are on the page as text, readable and
  searchable, which is the only reason a video page ever shows up in search.

Each film also appears in more than one place, doing a different job each time:
the budget film introduces the buyer path, the property films demonstrate your
marketing to sellers, and the buyer series is both a sequence and five separate
entry points.

## Honest about what is not finished

This is the part most agencies leave out, so it is here in the open.

**The site does not yet say your licensed title, and it will not until you
confirm it.** New York requires it on advertising. Guessing at a licence detail
is a regulatory problem, not a copy problem, so the site states what it knows —
Homes R Us Realty, the office address, the phone number — and openly records
what it does not.

Wherever something is not confirmed, the site says so on the page, in small
type, next to the claim. That includes:

- the client reviews, until you have written permission to use them in
  marketing;
- the transcripts, which were generated from the audio and need one read-through
  (they are good, but they misheard "Brehaut" as "Bregaud");
- the closing dates, which none of the review screenshots showed;
- one genuine conflict: the review for 181 Meisner reads like a buyer wrote it,
  while your own film describes relisting and selling it. Both may be true, but
  the site does not guess which side you were on.

**Nothing needs a developer to fix any of this.** Every one of them is a value
in one file. Supply the details and they appear.

## What it costs to run

Nothing.

- Hosting: free, on GitHub Pages.
- No database, no subscriptions, no plugins to renew, no monthly platform fee.
- No form, so no spam and no data to protect.
- The site collects nothing about visitors — no cookies, no analytics, no
  tracking. There is no cookie banner because there is nothing to consent to.

Adding a new film later is: drop the file in, run one command, add a few lines.
Adding a new closed sale is one entry in one file. The site rebuilds and deploys
itself.

---

## What it is not

Being straight about the limits:

- **Not a listing search.** It does not connect to the MLS and cannot show live
  inventory. Building that is a licensed product and a separate project.
- **Not a lead form.** Contact is call, text, and email. That is deliberate — a
  form on this kind of hosting cannot be secured properly, and a phone call
  converts better than a form submission anyway.
- **Not a guarantee of ranking.** The site is built correctly for local search,
  but the biggest lever is your Google Business Profile, which only you can
  claim. That list is in `docs/seo-strategy.md`.
